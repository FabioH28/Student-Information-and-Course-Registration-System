Documentation Strategy

The pack follows a practical multi-view architecture approach. Instead of relying on a single big diagram, it documents the system through complementary viewpoints: context, use cases, conceptual domain, database structure, service architecture, deployment, and runtime behavior.

How to Explain the Full System Professionally

Functional view

Actors and core workflows

CIS serves three primary operational actors: students, teachers, and administrators. Students consume academic, financial, and campus life services. Teachers handle teaching operations such as attendance and grading. Administrators govern institutional data, scheduling, finance, and communication.

Domain view

Bounded contexts

The solution is best explained through four bounded contexts: academic administration, finance, student engagement, and identity & access. That framing makes the domain model and ER design much easier to justify.

Architecture view

Layered full-stack architecture

The implemented stack follows a clean pattern: React handles user interaction, FastAPI handles orchestration and policy enforcement, and MySQL stores durable institutional records. This separation is easy to defend in maintainability and testability discussions.

Behavioral view

Critical runtime scenarios

Login, course registration, and grade publication were chosen as priority behavioral diagrams because they combine security, validation, concurrency, and business rules. These are the strongest scenarios to demonstrate engineering depth in presentation.
